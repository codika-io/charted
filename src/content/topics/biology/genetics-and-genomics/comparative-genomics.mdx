**Comparative Genomics** sits within genetics and genomics and addresses cross-species alignment, conservation, and inference of gene function from evolution. The page below sketches the conceptual scope of the area, the methodological tools it relies on, and the recent literature anchoring its current frontier.

The area organises around a small number of recurring axes: *scope* (what biological scales the work spans), *method* (the dominant experimental or computational tools), *data regime* (what kinds of measurements are now routine vs. still frontier), and *open questions* (what the field cannot yet do reliably). The sources below cover different combinations of these axes.

## Foundational references

*Strachan, Human Molecular Genetics* is a standard reference for the foundations covered here, used across the field to anchor terminology, canonical models, and the relationships between sub-areas of comparative genomics. Treat it as the entry point to which the more specialised work below adds frontier detail.

## Supporting context

Supporting context comes from *An integrated encyclopedia of DNA elements in the human genome* (Encode, 2012), cited here as a representative entry into adjacent results that reinforce the framing of comparative genomics without being the central methodological claim.

## Open questions

Open questions in comparative genomics cluster around scaling current methods to larger systems, integrating measurements across modalities, and producing predictive rather than descriptive models. The references above mark the work that the next iteration of this page should engage with in more specific detail.
