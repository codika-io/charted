**Visual System** sits within sensory systems and addresses retina to cortex — receptive fields, hierarchical processing, motion and depth. The page below sketches the conceptual scope of the area, the methodological tools it relies on, and the recent literature anchoring its current frontier.

The area organises around a small number of recurring axes: *scope* (what biological scales the work spans), *method* (the dominant experimental or computational tools), *data regime* (what kinds of measurements are now routine vs. still frontier), and *open questions* (what the field cannot yet do reliably). The sources below cover different combinations of these axes.

## Foundational references

*Kandel, Principles of Neural Science* is a standard reference for the foundations covered here, used across the field to anchor terminology, canonical models, and the relationships between sub-areas of visual system. Treat it as the entry point to which the more specialised work below adds frontier detail.

## Supporting context

Supporting context comes from *Receptive fields, binocular interaction and functional architecture in the cat's visual cortex* (Hubel et al., 1962), cited here as a representative entry into adjacent results that reinforce the framing of visual system without being the central methodological claim.

## Open questions

Open questions in visual system cluster around scaling current methods to larger systems, integrating measurements across modalities, and producing predictive rather than descriptive models. The references above mark the work that the next iteration of this page should engage with in more specific detail.
