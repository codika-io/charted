Biology covers life at every scale. At the molecular end, *biochemistry* and *molecular biology* describe the proteins, nucleic acids, and metabolic networks that make cells function. *Cell biology* and *structural biology* describe how those molecules assemble into the machinery of a cell. *Genetics and genomics* trace the information that determines a cell's identity and how that information varies across populations. *Immunology*, *microbiology*, and *neuroscience* describe specific systems where cells coordinate to defend, replicate, or compute. *Evolution and ecology* extend the same questions across populations and species over evolutionary time. *Systems biology* sits orthogonally to the others, building quantitative, predictive models of biological networks rather than dissecting individual pathways.

Twenty-first-century biology has been reshaped by a handful of methodological revolutions: high-throughput sequencing made every cell readable, cryo-electron microscopy turned protein structure determination into a near-routine pipeline, single-cell measurements replaced bulk averages with population distributions, and machine learning began to bridge the gap between sequence, structure, and function. The frontier topics under this branch each track a specific methodological cluster where these tools are producing recognisable communities and recent paper sets.
