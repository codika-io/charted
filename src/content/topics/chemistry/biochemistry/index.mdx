Biochemistry turns the chemical toolkit on the molecules of life: proteins, nucleic acids, lipids, carbohydrates, and the small-molecule metabolites that flow between them. Its central questions are structural (what three-dimensional shape does a biopolymer fold into, and how does that shape encode function?), catalytic (how do enzymes accelerate reactions by many orders of magnitude under mild conditions?), and systemic (how do thousands of coupled reactions sustain a living cell?). Modern biochemistry is increasingly quantitative and computational, with cryo-electron microscopy resolving protein complexes at near-atomic resolution, mass spectrometry mapping entire proteomes and metabolomes, and machine-learning models such as AlphaFold predicting structures from sequence alone.
