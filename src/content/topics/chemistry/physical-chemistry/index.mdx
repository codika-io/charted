Physical chemistry derives the rate laws, equilibria, and spectroscopic signatures that the rest of chemistry runs on. It sits between physics and chemistry: its objects are atoms, molecules, and condensed phases, but its tools are quantum mechanics, statistical mechanics, and thermodynamics. The discipline divides into *thermodynamics and statistical mechanics* (what reactions favour at equilibrium and why), *chemical kinetics and dynamics* (how fast they proceed and through which transition states), *spectroscopy* (how matter couples to electromagnetic radiation and what that reveals about structure and motion), and *electrochemistry* (the chemistry of electron transfer and ionic transport). Frontier work uses ultrafast laser pulses to watch chemical events on femtosecond timescales, single-molecule probes to resolve dynamics one event at a time, and ab initio simulation to predict reactivity from first principles.
