Materials chemistry sits at the engineering frontier of the discipline: the goal is no longer to understand an individual molecule but to compose many of them into a solid, a framework, a film, or a colloid whose *collective* properties — porosity, conductivity, magnetism, mechanical strength, photoresponse — solve a problem. The field spans porous frameworks (metal-organic frameworks, covalent organic frameworks, zeolites), low-dimensional crystals (graphene, transition-metal dichalcogenides, perovskites), nanocrystals and quantum dots, polymers and supramolecular assemblies, and hybrid organic-inorganic systems. Methodological progress comes from new synthetic handles (modulated synthesis, atomic-layer deposition, post-synthetic modification) and from in-situ characterisation that watches materials form and respond to their environment.
