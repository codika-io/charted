**Equilibrium Concepts.** Nash, correlated, and sequential equilibria.

## Foundations and canonical references

The standard treatments of equilibrium concepts approach the subject from complementary angles. Osborne, *A Course in Game Theory* (1994) is the anchor reference for the subject and lays out the core definitions, theorems, and worked examples that practitioners return to.

A number of supporting contributions sharpen specific aspects of equilibrium concepts or connect it to neighbouring problems. *Non-cooperative games* (Nash, 1951) contributes to this area as one of the supporting references that inform current practice.

Open methodological questions for equilibrium concepts include sharpening the bridges between foundational theory and computational practice, extending classical results to broader or more structured settings, and integrating the techniques surveyed above with adjacent mathematical disciplines.
